2. Who can use it

You need your own account with your school's learning platform, or your own school Google account for Google Classroom, to use Averages.io, and you may only sign in with your own account. Your school controls that account, so if your school removes it or turns off access for outside apps, Averages.io will stop working for you.

Anyone can use Averages.io in Incognito mode, which stores nothing about you on our servers: Sync Across Devices, connected apps (Canva, Google Drive and OneDrive) and notifications are off. Classic mode lets you turn those on, so it can store information about you and connect to other services, and you must be at least 13 years old to use it. We ask whether you are 13 or older before you sign in. If you are under 13, Averages.io stays in Incognito mode and Classic mode cannot be turned on. If we learn that someone under 13 is using Classic mode, we will move them to Incognito mode and delete what Classic mode stored.

Your account is for you alone. Do not share your sign-in with anyone, and keep it private. You are responsible for what happens while you are signed in. If you think someone else has used your account, sign out and let us know at help@averages.io.

3. What Averages.io does

Averages.io shows you information your school already makes available to you, such as your courses, grades, assignments and calendar, in a different layout. It reads this from your school's learning platform or Google Classroom. On your school's learning platform it can also turn in your work and send your messages, but only when you choose to. In Classic mode it can also send your assignment files to apps you connect, like Canva, Google Drive and OneDrive, and send you browser notifications if you turn them on.

You are responsible for what you turn in and send through Averages.io, just as if you had done it on your school's platform yourself. If a turn-in or a message fails, Averages.io tells you, but it cannot promise that your teacher received it, so check your school's platform for anything that matters, especially before a deadline.

It is not your school's official record. If anything in Averages.io differs from what your school or teacher shows you, your school's version is the correct one.

4. Projected grades

Projected grades are estimates worked out from the trend in your recent graded work. They are not something your school has calculated, and they are not a promise of any final grade. Please do not rely on them for decisions that matter.
