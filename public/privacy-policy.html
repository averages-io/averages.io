Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 7, 2026

Averages.io shows your classes, grades and assignments from your school's learning platform or Google Classroom in one app, lets you turn in work and send messages on your school's platform, and lets you open your assignment files in Canva, Google Drive or OneDrive. This page explains exactly what it reads, what it keeps, where it keeps it and for how long.

The short version

  • Your grades, assignments and other schoolwork are never stored on our servers. They pass through to your device and are gone when you close the tab.
  • Averages.io only changes something on your school's platform when you ask it to: turning in work you chose, or sending a message you wrote.
  • The few things our servers do store are off until you turn them on, stay in the United States, and are deleted when you turn them off.
  • Files you open in Google Drive or OneDrive go straight between your browser and Google or Microsoft. Our servers keep only your sign-in for them, encrypted, so you stay connected.
  • Your data is never sold, never used for ads and never used to train AI models.

Who we are

Averages.io is a free web app made by a student. The app runs at app.averages.io and its server at api.averages.io. Averages.io isn't made by, endorsed by or affiliated with your school, your school's learning platform, Google, Microsoft or Canva. Its code is public on GitHub, so anyone can check this page against what the app actually does.

Questions about this policy go to help@averages.io.

Your school's learning platform

Signing in on your school's own Schoology or Canvas page is coming soon, and this page will be updated before it launches. Until then, you can sign in with a personal API key from your school's learning platform. Averages.io uses your sign-in to read, only for you and only while you use the app:

  • Your profile: your name, email address and profile picture, to show who's signed in.
  • Your classes and grades: including grading categories, to show your grades, your gradebook, where each one is heading and a projected GPA.
  • Your assignments: titles, due dates, descriptions, attached files and what you've already turned in, to show what's due and what's done.
  • Your class files and folders: the files your teachers post and the folders they keep them in, for the Files and Materials pages.
  • Your class calendar and updates: events and the updates your teachers post, for Home and Calendar.
  • The people in your classes: your teachers' and classmates' names, and who you can message, for Contacts and Messages.
  • Your messages: to show your inbox and conversations.

Averages.io only changes something on your school's platform when you ask it to. It does two things there, each only when you click to do it:

  • Turning in work: the file you choose (from your device, Google Drive, OneDrive or a Canva design saved as a PDF) or the answer you type is sent to that assignment as your submission.
  • Sending messages: a message you write is sent to the people you chose, or as your reply in a conversation.

It never posts, comments, deletes anything or changes grades. Everything it reads or sends passes through our server and is not stored on our servers. A file you turn in, download or send to Canva passes through our server on the way and isn't kept.

Your API key is sealed with AES-256 encryption into a cookie that only our server can open. It isn't stored anywhere else. The cookie expires after 30 days, or as soon as you sign out.

Google

Averages.io uses Google in two separate ways. Each one asks for your permission in Google's own window first, and you can say no to either.

Google Classroom. If you sign in with Google, Averages.io asks Google for permission to read, from your Google account:

  • Your name, email address and profile picture, to show who's signed in.
  • Your Google Classroom classes and, if you allow it, class rosters, to show your class names and who teaches them. Rosters can include your classmates' names; Averages.io only uses them to find your teachers.
  • Coursework and, if you allow them, class materials, topics and announcements, to show what's assigned and posted in your classes, and how your teachers organize it.
  • Your own submissions and grades, to show your grades and projected grades.

Google lets you untick the permissions marked "if you allow"; those parts of the app then stay empty. All of this is read-only: Averages.io can't post, submit or change anything in Google Classroom, so you turn in Classroom work on Google Classroom itself. Like your school platform's data, it passes through our server to your device and is not stored on our servers. Your Google sign-in is sealed into the same encrypted cookie as above.

Google Drive. If you connect Google Drive, Averages.io asks for permission to see, create and edit only the Google Drive files you use with Averages.io, plus your name and email address to show which account is connected. It can't see anything else in your Drive. It uses this to:

  • save a copy of an assignment file into an "Averages.io" folder in your Drive,
  • turn a Word, PowerPoint or Excel file into a Google Doc, Slides or Sheet when you choose Edit in Google Drive,
  • list the files it made, or that you picked, on the Files page,
  • open Google's file picker when you choose Add from Google Drive, and
  • turn in one of those files when you choose it for an assignment.

Files go from your browser straight into your Drive. So that you only connect once, our servers keep your Google Drive sign-in (encrypted, tied to your account, in the United States) and give your browser a short-lived pass when you use Google Drive. It's used for nothing else, and Disconnect deletes it. A file you turn in goes from your browser, through our server, to your school's platform, and isn't kept.

How Averages.io uses Google user data

Averages.io's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Google user data is only used to provide the features described above. It is never used for advertising, never sold or given to data brokers or anyone else, never used to train or improve AI or machine-learning models, and never used to decide anyone's credit or eligibility for anything. Nobody at Averages.io reads it.

Microsoft OneDrive

If you connect OneDrive, Averages.io asks Microsoft for permission to:

  • Sign you in and read your basic profile (your name and email address), to show which account is connected.
  • Stay connected for up to a day without asking you to sign in again.
  • Read and write files in its own app folder, Apps/Averages.io in your OneDrive.
  • Read your files, only if you choose Add from OneDrive. Averages.io asks for this separately, the first time you use Add from OneDrive, so you can browse your OneDrive and pick files. Only the files you pick are listed or used.

Averages.io uses this to save copies of assignment files into its folder, to list those and the files you picked on the Files page, and to turn in a file you choose for an assignment. Like Google Drive, files go straight between your browser and OneDrive, and our servers keep your Microsoft sign-in (encrypted, tied to your account, in the United States) so you only connect once. Disconnect deletes it. A file you turn in passes through our server on its way to your school's platform and isn't kept.

Canva

If you connect Canva, Averages.io asks Canva for permission to read your Canva name, see your designs and folders, create new designs, save a design as a PDF, and make, rename and move folders. When you choose Edit in Canva, our server copies that assignment file from your school's platform into your Canva account. When you turn in a Canva design, Canva makes a PDF of it, which passes through our server to your assignment and isn't kept. On the Files page you can browse your Canva folders, make a folder, rename one and move designs between them. Averages.io never deletes anything in your Canva account. To make all this work, our server keeps, for your account only:

  • your Canva sign-in, encrypted so that it only opens for your account,
  • your Canva display name,
  • your Drafts list: which assignment file became which Canva design, and
  • for one day at most, which page to send you back to when you click Return in Canva.

Your designs and folders themselves live in your own Canva account.

Browser notifications

If you turn on notifications in Settings (Classic mode only), Averages.io can let you know when a grade or an assignment is posted, an assignment is due within a day, you get a message, or a teacher posts an update. To check for you while the app is closed, our server keeps, for your account only:

  • your browser's notification address, for up to 5 browsers, which your browser gives Averages.io when you allow notifications,
  • which kinds of notification you turned on,
  • your sign-in, encrypted with a separate key that only this feature uses, and only until your sign-in would have expired anyway, and
  • a fingerprint of what it last saw: short scrambled codes, never a grade, an assignment title or a name, so it can tell when something is new.

About every 20 minutes it reads only what those notifications need and compares. If something changed, it sends a short notice like "A new grade was posted." The notice never includes the grade, the assignment or who sent a message. It is delivered by your browser's own push service (run by Google, Mozilla, Apple or Microsoft, depending on your browser). Turning notifications off, switching to Incognito mode or signing out deletes all of it immediately.

What's kept on your device

Some things are kept in your own browser so the app works and loads quickly. None of it is sent to our servers unless you turn on Sync Across Devices.

  • Your sign-in cookie, described above.
  • Your settings and appearance, like your theme, colors, background image, course nicknames and the profile name and photo you set, in your browser's local storage.
  • Schoolwork already loaded, held in that browser tab so moving between pages is instant. It's discarded when you close the tab. Your class files list, and extra details some pages load (like your teachers, events and folders), are kept for up to 5 minutes.
  • Google Drive and OneDrive, a short-lived pass from our servers while the page is open. Which account is connected, your Google Drive drafts list and the OneDrive files you picked stay on that device until you sign out or disconnect. If a different student signs in on the same device, all of this is cleared.

If you upload a profile photo or a background image, it's checked for inappropriate content on your own device before it's used. The image is never uploaded anywhere for that check.

What our servers store

Our servers store data for four features, and only after you turn them on. All four are stored with Cloudflare in the United States, in a separate storage space for each student, and all four are off in Incognito mode. Nothing else about you is kept.

  • Sync Across Devices (off by default) stores your user ID, the profile you entered (name, email address and, if you set one, a profile photo), your appearance settings (accent color, light or dark mode and, if you uploaded one, a background image) and your app settings, so they carry over when you sign in elsewhere. Course nicknames and course order are not included. Turning it off deletes everything it stored immediately.
  • Canva (only if you connect it) stores what's listed in the Canva section above. Disconnecting deletes all of it immediately.
  • Google Drive and OneDrive (only if you connect them) store your sign-in for each (encrypted, so it only opens for your account), the email address and name of the account you connected, and what you allowed. Never your files. Disconnecting deletes it immediately.
  • Browser notifications (off by default) store what's listed in the Browser notifications section above. Turning them off deletes all of it immediately.

Cloudflare, which runs our servers, also keeps short technical logs of requests to our server (for example the time, the address requested, your IP address and whether it worked). We use them only to fix problems and keep the service secure, and they're deleted after a few days (7 at most). They never contain your grades or files. To stop abuse, our server also counts requests from each IP address or account for a minute or so, in memory only; those counts are never saved.

Schools that contact us

If your school's IT team emails schools@averages.io or applies at app.averages.io/schools/apply, we keep what they send us: the school or district's name, its Canvas address, the email address to reply to, and a contact name and note if they give them. Emails to schools@averages.io are copied to the inbox of the person who runs Averages.io, and we remember for 30 days which addresses got our automatic reply, so nobody gets it twice. This is information about schools, not students. It's kept with Cloudflare in the United States while we work with that school, and deleted if the school asks at help@averages.io. The application form uses Cloudflare Turnstile to check that a person is sending it; Cloudflare's own privacy policy covers what that check looks at. To confirm the email address is really theirs, the form can email it a 6-digit code. We keep only a scrambled (hashed) copy of that code, for 10 minutes, and the times codes were sent to that address for 10 minutes, so nobody can flood it with codes.

Who we share data with

  • Nobody who would use it for themselves. We don't sell, rent or trade your data, and we don't share it with advertisers or data brokers.
  • Cloudflare runs our servers and stores the data described above, in the United States, only to provide Averages.io.
  • Your school's learning platform, Google, Microsoft and Canva receive your requests only to do what you asked, like reading your classes, turning in a file you chose or sending your message. Each has its own privacy policy.
  • Your browser's push service (Google, Mozilla, Apple or Microsoft) delivers notifications, if you turn them on. It sees only the short notice described above.
  • If the law requires it, we would disclose what we have. Because we keep so little, there is usually nothing to give.

How we protect your data

  • Everything travels over encrypted HTTPS connections.
  • Your school sign-in is sealed with AES-256 encryption in a cookie your browser's scripts can't read.
  • Canva, Google Drive and OneDrive sign-ins are encrypted when stored, and tied to your account. The sign-in kept for notifications is encrypted with its own separate key.
  • Stored data stays in the United States, in a separate storage space for each student.
  • Averages.io asks for the narrowest permissions that work: on your school's platform, reading plus the two things you do yourself (turning in and messages); read-only access to Google Classroom; only the Google Drive files you use with it; and only its own OneDrive folder, unless you choose files to add.
  • The code is public, so it can be checked by anyone. Please report security problems to help@averages.io.

How long data is kept, and deleting it

  • Schoolwork from your school's platform or Google Classroom: never stored on our servers, and gone from your browser when you close the tab.
  • Your sign-in cookie: 30 days, or until you sign out.
  • Sync Across Devices: until you turn it off in Settings.
  • Canva: until you click Disconnect in Settings > Integrations. Return information: one day at most.
  • Browser notifications: until you turn them off, switch to Incognito mode or sign out.
  • Google Drive and OneDrive sign-ins: until you click Disconnect in Settings > Integrations, switch to Incognito mode, or Google or Microsoft ends them.
  • Server logs: 7 days at most.
  • Files you saved to Google Drive, OneDrive or Canva are yours, and stay in those accounts until you delete them there. Work you turned in and messages you sent are on your school's platform, like anything you do there yourself.
  • Schools' applications and emails: while we work with that school. Automatic-reply records: 30 days.

To remove Averages.io's access completely, also remove it in that service's own account settings: your Google Account's third-party connections, your Microsoft account's app permissions, or Canva's connected apps. To stop notifications in your browser as well, block them for app.averages.io in your browser's settings. To ask about or delete anything we store, email help@averages.io.

Incognito and Classic modes

Averages.io has two modes. In Incognito mode, nothing about you is stored on our servers: Sync Across Devices, connected apps (Canva, Google Drive and OneDrive) and notifications are off, and Canva, Google Drive and OneDrive are never contacted. In Classic mode, you can turn those features on, one at a time. Switching to Incognito turns them all off and deletes what they stored. Your grades and assignments are handled the same way in both modes: they pass through to your device and are never stored on our servers.

Children

Averages.io is made for middle and high school students. Before you sign in, it asks whether you're 13 or older. If you're under 13, Averages.io stays in Incognito mode, and Classic mode can't be turned on, because that answer is sealed into your sign-in. If we learn that someone under 13 has used a feature that stores data, we'll delete what it stored. Parents and guardians can email help@averages.io with any question or request.

Do Not Track

Averages.io does not track you across other websites, and it does not use advertising or analytics trackers. Your browser's Do Not Track setting does not change how Averages.io works, because there is no tracking for it to turn off.

We will never sell your data

Averages.io will never sell your academic data or personal information to anyone, and there is no option to enable this. It doesn't show ads, and it doesn't use your data to train AI models.

Changes to this policy

If Averages.io changes what it reads, stores or shares, this page and the date at the top are updated first. If a change affects how your Google data is used, you'll be told in the app, and asked for permission again, before it applies.
